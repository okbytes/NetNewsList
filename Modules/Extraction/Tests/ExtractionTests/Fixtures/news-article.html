Towns Pool Money for Water Repairs

By Dana Rivera

The committee met on Tuesday to review the findings of the eighteen-month study, which examined how small towns adapt their water systems to longer dry seasons.

Researchers interviewed operators in forty-two municipalities and found that the most effective changes were often the least expensive: fixing leaks, metering, and sharing equipment across town lines.

Several engineers said the hardest part was not the technology but coordination, because neighboring districts rarely share budgets, schedules, or even the same maps of their own pipes.

The report recommends a regional fund that towns could draw on for emergency repairs, paired with a shared training program for new operators who are entering the field each year.

Critics argue that the fund would favor larger towns with grant writers on staff, and they asked the committee to reserve a portion of the money for the smallest systems.

A vote on the proposal is expected next month, after a public comment period that will include hearings in each of the affected counties and an online form for written feedback.

Officials said the regional approach could serve as a model elsewhere.
