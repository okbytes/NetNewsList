Night Trains Return to Europe

Sleeper services that disappeared a decade ago are running again on several routes, and operators say demand has surprised them. Travelers cite comfort, arrival in city centers, and a smaller carbon footprint than flying.

The new carriages include private compartments with showers, a change from the shared couchettes that many riders remember. Prices remain higher than budget airlines on most routes, although operators expect that gap to narrow.

Rail advocates hope the revival will push governments to fund cross-border track upgrades, which would shorten journeys and make overnight schedules easier to plan across three or more countries.
